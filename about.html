--- title: About bodyclass: about page ---

The project understands ARTivism as the intentional convergence of artistic practice and activism. In ARTivism, aesthetic creation becomes a form of social participation: artists use the visual, performative, embodied, or affective possibilities of art to intervene in public conversations and contribute to processes of change. Their work may expose forms of exclusion, challenge dominant representations, recover histories that have been marginalized or erased, create spaces for collective memory, or make other ways of imagining social life visible. ARTivism can emerge through photography, performance, film, installation, digital media, muralism, and community-based practices, among many other forms. What defines it is the purposeful relationship between artistic creation and social engagement. An artwork that addresses a political subject is therefore not automatically ARTivism; the term points to practices in which aesthetic choices are consciously connected to intervention, participation, or collective action.

Global ARTivism approaches these practices through the framework of the public humanities. The repository creates a space where artistic work can be encountered alongside historical and cultural context, artist perspectives, scholarly interpretation, and materials generated through teaching and public programs. Knowledge develops through these connections. Artists contribute their perspectives on their own practices, while students and educators can participate through research, translation, interviews, curatorial interpretation, documentation, and the creation of pedagogical materials. The archive consequently continues to evolve as new voices and collaborations enter the project. Although based at UVA, Global ARTivism is international in scope. The university serves as a site of encounter where artists, students, educators, and community members can come together through courses, exhibitions, residencies, workshops, and conversations. The digital repository extends those exchanges beyond the physical and temporal boundaries of a particular event, allowing them to remain accessible and to generate new connections over time. Language is integral to this work. Whenever possible, materials are made available in more than one language, beginning with English and Spanish, with translation recognized as an intellectual and interpretive contribution to the project. Collaboration also shapes the ethics of the archive. Global ARTivism seeks to build sustained relationships with artists and contributors and to recognize their agency in how their work and knowledge are represented. Artist voice, informed permission, careful contextualization, accessibility, contributor attribution, and respect for intellectual-property and image rights guide the repository’s editorial practices. In this way, the digital archive preserves the intellectual and creative exchanges generated through encounters with artists while opening them to future teaching, research, and public engagement.
